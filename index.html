Executive summary: The cost of being invisible on Google

Proposal premise: The business does not simply need more website visitors. It needs a discoverable, credible acquisition channel capable of bringing qualified organizations into conversations about AI services.

AI-Rebooted offers AI-related services and positions itself around improving productivity and reducing risk. Yet preliminary SpyFu research indicates that its website currently has very limited visibility in Google organic search.

That matters because a typical 100-person client generates $3,500 in monthly revenue for AI-Rebooted—or $42,000 annually if retained for 12 months. Being absent from relevant search results creates a meaningful potential customer acquisition gap.

The central business case: One new client with 12 months of payments generates $42,000 in gross revenue versus an annual SEO fee of $11,964 at the proposed startup rate. That is approximately 3.5× the SEO fee in gross revenue, before servicing costs, acquisition delays, or any other expenses.

This proposal establishes a 90-day foundation for organic search growth and defines ongoing SEO services at a startup-friendly rate.

Important distinction: These are revenue-opportunity scenarios, not verified lost contracts. SEO cannot guarantee rankings, leads, or sales. Direct website analytics and customer conversion data are needed to estimate actual losses.

3. Google Business Profile: Verify and address the gap

An active, well-managed Google Business Profile (GBP) has not yet been independently verified for AI-Rebooted. Do not represent the profile as definitively missing until this check is complete.

A GBP can help eligible businesses appear in Google Maps and local business results, potentially increasing trust and discovery for geographic-intent queries.

Scope

  1. Check whether a GBP already exists, including duplicate/unclaimed listings.
  2. Confirm eligibility: Google typically requires in-person customer interaction at an eligible location or through in-person service-area visits; online-only businesses are generally ineligible.
  3. If eligible, claim/create and verify the profile, choose accurate categories, complete business details, list legitimate services/service areas, and link the website.
  4. Add appropriate images and establish a compliant, genuine customer-review request process.
  5. Monitor profile engagement and maintain accurate information.

If the business is online-only or otherwise ineligible, do not create a misleading listing. Instead, prioritize organic search, industry directories, partnerships, and other valid business discovery channels.
